Practise

Play your own instrument and get feedback on each phrase.

Practice listens to your instrument through the microphone and follows the piece you chose, note by note. It then tells you what was right and what was not. It listens only for the notes of the piece that is loaded.

Practise an exercise

The quickest way to start, and a good way to check your microphone.

  1. Open Practice and choose or practise an exercise. It is also in the settings drawer.
  2. Pick an exercise: C major scale, C major arpeggio or Twinkle, Twinkle.
  3. Pick a tempo (60, 80 or 100 bpm) and a starting octave. Higher octaves are detected more accurately.
  4. Choose Use this exercise, then tap the microphone.
  5. A four-beat count-in appears on screen. Play your first note on the beat after "4" and keep time with the pulsing dot.
  6. After each bar a feedback card appears.

Play along with a score

  1. Find a piece in Search and open it. It appears in Practice.
  2. Tap the microphone. The first time, choose a tempo: Slow (60%), Steady (80%) or As written. On a piano score you can also choose which hand or hands you will play. The chip at the top of the score changes them later.
  3. A one-bar count-in follows. Start on the first note.
  4. As you play, each note turns soft green when it was right, and red when it was a wrong note, missed, or badly early or late. The page scrolls to keep the next note in view.
  5. A line at the top says what went wrong, for example Played D instead of D♯ · bar 12.

The score follows your tempo. If you slow down or speed up it keeps up, and it waits if you stop.

Feedback on each phrase

Every few seconds the server looks at the last phrase and says how it went: on pitch, roughly there or off pitch, with notes on specific bars. A bar with no mistakes gets a friendly Nice, that bar was clean.

It is a guide, not a teacher. The feedback is produced automatically and can be wrong. Use it to spot where to slow down and listen, not as the last word on your playing.

Marking up the score

Use the pen to draw on the score, and Clear annotations to wipe it. What you draw stays on your Mac.

Getting good results

  • Use a quiet room, and place the microphone close to the instrument.
  • Play clear, single notes at first, at a tempo you can manage.
  • If everything shows red, check the Practice tips.

What is sent

Your playing is analysed on your Mac. Only the notes the app detected (their pitch, timing and loudness) and the title of the piece go to the server so it can write the feedback. The recording itself is not sent.
