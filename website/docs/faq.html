Questions and answers

Cost, privacy, instruments, open source.

Short answers. Longer ones are in the guides.

Is it free?

The app is free to download and use. There are no ads and no purchases in it.

Which computers does it run on?

Macs running macOS 12 or later, Apple silicon or Intel. Other platforms are not available yet.

Why does macOS warn me about it?

The app is not signed with an Apple Developer ID, which needs a paid Apple account. The code is open source and the build that made your download is public, and you can check your download with its checksum. See Getting started.

Do I need an account?

No. The app has no accounts, and does not ask for your name or email address.

Does it work offline?

No. Songs, practice feedback, the library and the quiz all come from the server, so you need an internet connection.

Which instruments does it work with?

It listens to the sound of a real instrument and follows the piece you chose. It has been built and tested mostly with piano and guitar recordings, so other instruments may be less accurate. A clear, single line of notes in a quiet room works best.

Is my playing recorded?

No. Your playing is analysed on your Mac, and only the notes it detected (pitch, timing and loudness) and the piece's title are sent to the server. The recording is not sent.

What happens to my hum recordings?

A hum is sent to the server to make your song, and deleted automatically after about three hours.

Who sees my Talk mode requests?

Only if you agree in the consent box: Google Gemini sees your request and a description of your song, and ElevenLabs sees your voice if you speak. Talk mode is for people 18 or older. See Talk mode.

Can I use the songs it makes?

The songs made from your hum are for you to use, and we do not claim ownership of them. Because they are generated automatically, we cannot promise they are free of similarity to other music. The Terms of Service (linked in the footer) have the details.

Where do the scores come from, and why no modern songs?

From PDMX, a public-domain collection. We screen it and offer only music whose composer lived long enough ago for it to be in the public domain, so modern songs are left out. See Library and shelf.

How do I delete my data?

There is no account to delete. Your shelf, hummed songs and notes are stored on your Mac only, so removing the app's data removes them. Hum recordings on the server delete themselves.

How do I report a bug, a wrong score, or send feedback?

Open Settings › Contact support or send feedback in the app. For a wrong score, tell us the title and composer.

Is it open source?

Yes. The code is under the MIT License, on GitHub. The scores and other third-party parts keep their own licenses.
